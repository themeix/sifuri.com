<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">Seth Godin notes that when the first thing you do is measure a new opportunity against <a href="http://sethgodin.typepad.com/seths_blog/2012/06/whats-on-your-list.html">the list of why you can&#8217;t</a>, then the list is the most important thing in your life. An economist would call this revealed preferences.</p>
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;"> </p>
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;"> </p>
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">Yep, my kids prevent me from giving up entirely when life seems unbearable, because quitting would be worse. There&#8217;s always a &#8220;worse:&#8221; know what yours is. I know it&#8217;s really freaking unlikely that I&#8217;ll ever be as broken down as I was walking up the damn TVD that day in Mountains, when I was reduced to setting goals of a few yards, then a few feet, then a few steps, and then one at a time.</p>
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767; min-height: 15.0px;"> </p>
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">For some, the &#8220;worse&#8221; is physical, emotional, mental, spiritual. But we all have our bottom lines. When you get close to that bastard, bend your knees and try to push off. Use yours like a trampoline, giving you energy and confidence and faith from remembering what&#8217;s important, from brushing up against the greatest part of your life. </p>
