<p>Confession: I bought an iPad, the smallest wifi-only version. I wanted something that was more functional than my Blackberry <a href="http://www.thoughtstorm.com/500-software-bounty-for-blackberry-bcc-tool-increased/">Pearl</a> but with a more convenient form factor than my laptop for use during parts of my commute.</p>
<p>Here&#8217;s the question: I&#8217;ve been using <a href="https://basecamp.com/">Basecamp</a> for internal (and now external) projects, and I had been of the impression that I would/should get an app to work with that site. I&#8217;d seen lists of iphone apps, including basecamp&#8217;s own <a href="http://37signals.com/extras">list</a>.</p>
<p>But I was trying to get a handle on which one to get; review seem scarce, and the app store model makes it impossible to try unless the maker has created a &#8220;lite&#8221; version (see <a href="http://headquartersapp.com/">Headquarters</a> and their lite version: the app framework might be one where what used to be called &#8220;crippleware&#8221; doesn&#8217;t seem to be so bad.)</p>
<p>But last night, on a whim that seemed silly at the time, I just fired up the <a href="https://basecamp.com/">Basecamp</a> website to see how broken it would be. Pleasant surprise: it worked pretty darn fine, just like on my laptop. Now, in fairness, I did identify one missing feature: the ability to rearrange tasks in a to-do list was missing (the little 4-way arrow was not there at all). Maybe that&#8217;s the dreaded no-flash problem. I can deal.</p>
<p>What this makes me think about, though, is a couple things: first, I just saved $10 or $12; second, those app companies are definitely confined to a niche; third, apps that access web services have their days numbered as mobile browsers improve (or, in the case of the iPad, screen sizes and keyboard functions).</p>
<p>Where these apps would create an immediate urge to buy would be if they mimicked &#8220;desktop&#8221; add-ons to Basecamp, such that they could operate offline and upload/sync my changes later. True, part of that feature needs to be implemented in the underlying web service, but wifi and 3G haven&#8217;t eliminated the need for the mobile professional to be functional when there&#8217;s no connection.</p>
<p><em>[Ed.: followup&#8211;  <a href="http://www.outpostapp.com/">Outpost</a> claims &#8220;full offline capabilities.&#8221; That&#8217;s the secret sauce; I think this app will make the cut for a full-fledged review/shootout.]</em></p>
<p>At ThoughtStorm [http://thoughtstorm.com/], we were fans of <a href="http://office.microsoft.com/en-us/groove/FX100487641033.aspx">Groove</a> (it still lives on my several machines, in fact) because of the online/offline capability. Until a client accepts my excuse of &#8220;I couldn&#8217;t work on it because I couldn&#8217;t get a connection in the subway or on the plane,&#8221; people like me will always have a need to maintain local/offline access to our materials, even if they are backed up to, or even exist primarily, in the cloud. (See more on this concern in my <a href="http://rickcolosimo.com/category/reviews/">review</a> of <a href="http://rickcolosimo.com/ready-fire-review-of-plan-plus-online/">PlanPlus Online</a>.</p>
<p>When do you decide that you want/need an app vs. just using the website? There are tools to &#8220;make an app&#8221; from your blog. Does this make sense? Do you use these apps yourself to read favorite blogs?</p>
