<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">I recently read a post that pushed <a href="http://www.tonywright.com/2010/freemium-founders-start-charging-for-things-today/">&#8220;freemium&#8221; companies to start charging</a> right away &#8212; to put the paid offering on the table to see who will buy how much at what price. It&#8217;s good advice, of course, but really in many ways just an okay read; nothing you haven&#8217;t already read somewhere else.</p>
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">But I had a different thought for the launch of <a href="http://CEOguides.com">CEOguides.com</a>: why not start at the paid end of the market (where we&#8217;re at: professional services), and go down, rather than start at the bottom and climb up. You might call it mass customization (very loosely), or even a Targetization (bringing design to the mass market).</p>
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">We have always been in the couture, bespoke, handcrafted, artisanal knowledge &amp; advice business. (Nobody pays 3 figures/hour for cookie cutter advice!) Our strategy for these new service-related businesses should mirror that.</p>
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">Premifree? Pree? Not so catchy; perhaps I&#8217;ll keep working on a label that fits.</p>
<p style="margin: 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px 0.0px; font: 12.0px 'Lucida Grande'; color: #676767;">What examples do you have of startup companies taking this reverse approach rather than the freemium model?</p>
