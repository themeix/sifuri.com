<blockquote><p>Are you truly committed – or just curious?<br />
Are you truly committed – or just involved?<br />
Are you truly committed – or just concerned?<br />
Are you truly committed – or just interested?<br />
Are you truly committed – or just legally obligated?<br />
Are you truly committed – or just not unhappy enough to change?<br />
Are you truly committed – or just biding your time until something better comes along?</p></blockquote>
<p>Excerpt from a post on showing the world <a href="http://www.hellomynameisblog.com/2010/12/what-every-young-leader-should-know.html">you&#8217;re serious</a>.</p>
<p>As many of you know, I divorced my wife a month ago (November 15, 2010). I know me: someday I&#8217;ll get married again. And the experience has caused me to be a little more deliberate about some things, and this set of questions really hit home.</p>
<p>I want the person I&#8217;m with to be committed to me because that&#8217;s who I am: a committed person &#8212; no doubts, no halfway gestures, no hedging. I don&#8217;t play poker that often but I know that I&#8217;m reasonably good because I&#8217;m never afraid to go all-in.</p>
<p>In love, that&#8217;s always been me (and my bigger issue has been the equivalent of folding early (to continue the metaphor), of deciding that the future isn&#8217;t likely to develop the way I want and thus decide not to waste anyone&#8217;s time); in life, I&#8217;ve been happiest at work when I&#8217;ve been engaged, really engaged, in my work because it was important, interesting, challenging, and stretched me. I&#8217;ve been unhappiest when it&#8217;s been unimportant and when there&#8217;s no growth.</p>
<p>(As I post this, I think that I&#8217;m going to have to actually meet Scott Ginsberg sometime and see whether there&#8217;s any &#8220;there&#8221; there. Maybe he really is bright and insightful, or maybe he writes so much that something will make sense, or maybe it&#8217;s all bland enough that it will sound good to someone somewhere sometime.)</p>
