<p>So I was just watching a video of &#8220;some guy&#8221; (deliberately vague now because I&#8217;m going to sort of make fun of him in a minute) talking about how he uses Scrivener on his Mac for writing.</p>
<p>But he pronounced it with a long /i/, like it rhymed with &#8216;hive:&#8217; scrive-ner, as opposed to scriv-ner. I&#8217;d honestly never ever heard it pronounced that way, and given the number of kids in my 11th or 12th grade <a href="https://www.greececsd.org/Page/13">English class</a> reading <a href="http://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11231">Bartleby</a>, I&#8217;m sure someone would have corrected all of us if we were saying it wrong. However, the average verbal SAT of that class was probably above 650 (and I&#8217;m old-ish, so that&#8217;s like a 730 today; look up the <a href="http://www.wsj.com/articles/SB124397818883378713">scaled score conversion</a>) &#8212; I&#8217;m guessing we mostly had it right.</p>
<p>Anyhow, I won&#8217;t name the fellow or link or anything just because he pronounced it funny. But I am <a href="https://fiveisenough.wordpress.com/2007/08/16/blank-page/">unsure</a> about whether I want to watch the rest of this little video.</p>
<p>But I will because I just started using <a href="http://www.literatureandlatte.com/scrivener.php">Scrivener</a> and would like to see how other folks do it.</p>
<p>So here&#8217;s the quote (I really don&#8217;t have a source for this; sorry):</p>
<blockquote><p>It&#8217;s better to keep your mouth shut and be thought a fool then to open it and remove all doubt.</p></blockquote>
<p>And yes, my visceral reaction to people mispronouncing things does have some small relation to my mother&#8217;s penchant for talking about the &#8220;Gonzilla&#8221; movies that I watched on Saturday afternoons as a kid. Yes, they were in fact dubbed &#8220;Godzilla&#8221; movies. Oh well &#8212; some things stay with you.</p>
