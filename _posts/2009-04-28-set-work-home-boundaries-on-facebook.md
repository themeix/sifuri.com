<p>A breakfast <a href="http://rickcolosimo.com/improve-your-personal-branding-by-separating-your-blogs/">networking group</a> I attend regularly returns to the topic of social networking tools/sites and where people can or should draw lines to separate their business and personal lives. It&#8217;s a common sentiment that &#8220;I don&#8217;t want clients seeing old college photos.&#8221;</p>
<div style="width: 334px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><a href="http://www.newsoftheworld.co.uk/"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="  " title="Michael Phelps?" src="http://www.newsoftheworld.co.uk/" alt="Michael Phelps in a compromising position" width="324" height="432" /></a><p class="wp-caption-text">Michael Phelps in a compromising position</p></div>
<p>This <a href="http://www.thepersonalbrandingblog.com/2009/04/when-the-boss-invades-facebook.html">article</a> via the Personal Branding Blog deals expressly with these issues by attacking difficult question #1: What do you do when your boss sends you a friend request?</p>
<p>The short answer, that I was pleased to learn, is that you can use the friend &#8220;lists&#8221; feature. Create a friend list called &#8220;professional&#8221; and add the right folks to it. You can then edit your <a href="http://www.facebook.com/privacy/">privacy settings</a> and set permissions the way you want and then use the list as an exception to the viewing permissions. There are probably a variety of permutations you&#8217;ll have to sort through, and so you should test your settings vigorously before putting up <a href="http://www.newsoftheworld.co.uk/">something </a>that you <a href="http://nypost.com/2009/02/01/michael-phelps-caught-with-bong/">don&#8217;t want</a> people to see.</p>
