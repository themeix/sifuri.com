<p>I recently saw a question asking &#8220;Which is worse: flag burning or book burning?&#8221; My answer is definitely books, as in not even close for me.</p>
<p>My thoughts on the flag go back to some Army experiences &#8212; one in basic training where the guy who bunked next to me got nearly apoplectic when a faux Cuban officer was holding a lighter near a flag (that block of instruction, &#8220;Threat,&#8221; was absolutely the silliest thing I&#8217;d ever seen. But Cornelius&#8217;s eye nearly popped out of his head. The other thing that resonates for me is watching the Somalis drag the pilot&#8217;s body through the streets in 93 after the Black Hawk Down episode (another story on that too &#8212; have you seen the movie?). To me, I&#8217;d rather people burn flags than soldiers. I think of  those guys as living breathing flags.</p>
<p>And yep, my answer was a little aggressive. I guess I think of it as a way to get people to think about the point of the flag &#8212; it&#8217;s a symbol. And to me, just like the reason we shouldn&#8217;t torture terrorists, we should be pushing our beliefs at the time when they&#8217;re the most tested. Milton wrote &#8220;I will not praise a fugitive and cloistered virtue.&#8221; That line has stuck with me since college (Brit Lit. II, to be exact), and I find it has applied to dozens of situations over the years. I guess that&#8217;s why I think that flag burning has to be okay &#8212; it&#8217;s certainly more like speech than half the stuff we do protect under that umbrella.</p>
<p>Lines matter, both in the day to day (shouldn&#8217;t steal a pack of gum, shouldn&#8217;t spank your kids, shouldn&#8217;t lie to your boyfriend) and in the big picture (shouldn&#8217;t cheat on your wife, shouldn&#8217;t embezzle from the company, shouldn&#8217;t give up on your values). Respecting lines, throughout our lives, helps us. One of the most interesting cross-religious connections that I&#8217;ve made was when I was in the Middle East a few years ago during Ramadan, which  entails fasting from dawn to dusk over several weeks. A TV commentator described the importance of keeping the fast in almost the exact same terms I&#8217;d heard a year or two before that during a Lenten service in a Catholic church: keeping the fast gives us self-control against temptation from sin.</p>
<p>But this isn&#8217;t a class on religion, per se. It&#8217;s about how we decide to respect our own values, and how we help ourselves do that. It&#8217;s not always easy; if it were it probably wouldn&#8217;t matter all that much. But it becomes easier with practice and support until goals and values we&#8217;ve adopted really become part of us. And then we just know what is right and what is wrong.</p>
