<p>Here is another interesting take on the notion of not keeping all your <a href="http://diythemes.com/thesis/eggs-in-one-basket/">eggs in one basket</a>. The lesson is twofold: first, <em>spreading</em> your efforts is the same as <em>diluting</em> your efforts. That single vocabulary change really makes you stop and think. It slaps me in the face and I just came up with it.</p>
<p>But life moves on and the lessons aren&#8217;t always there at the beginning (multitasking used to be considered an asset; people put it on their resumes). So what do you do, today, when you stop and look at your life and there isn&#8217;t an obvious 10,000 hours of violin practice behind you already?</p>
<p>The alternatives:</p>
<ol>
<li>give up and accept that while you have built skills over the years, you haven&#8217;t become great at any one easily defined thing;</li>
<li>pick something that you think will fulfill you and dedicate the next 10,000 hours to that (5-10 years is not a big deal if you&#8217;re 25 &#8211; trust me &#8211; but it might be when 43 is two weeks away, or now, as I actually publish this, three weeks in the rearview mirror);</li>
<li>figure out how to think about what you&#8217;ve done in a way that captures your possible expertise in a brandable way that you&#8217;d like to continue.</li>
</ol>
<p>I&#8217;m opting for #3, which means I&#8217;ve got to push off a writing project to push the personal branding exercises to the shortlist. But I&#8217;m not sure I need, meaning I&#8217;m sure I don&#8217;t, to solve the personal brand question before finishing that project. (Wow, I&#8217;m glad I talked myself out of that detour.) That&#8217;s prioritization in real time &#8212; that&#8217;s exactly the stream of thoughts that just ran through my head.</p>
<p>I&#8217;ve learned the hard way that you can have too many priorities. But that&#8217;s a post for another day.</p>
