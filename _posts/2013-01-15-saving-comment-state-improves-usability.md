<p>If you want people to comment, say on a lifehacker <a href="http://lifehacker.com/5973033/how-i-turned-three-years-of-paper-into-a-highly-organized-searchable-document-database-in-two-days">post about going paperless</a>, and you require sign-ins for commenting, don&#8217;t trash their comments when they forget a password.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Among the least interesting passwords I remember is my lifehacker account for commenting. I don&#8217;t use it every day and it&#8217;s of zero value to a hacker (hence, a mediocre password such as &#8220;password&#8221;) to masquerade as me on lifehacker.</p>
<p>Today I wrote a brief comment on the post regarding scanning/ocr/storage workflow, contributing to the community in some small way. Lifehacker wants me to sign in. Of course, I forgot my password (since it&#8217;s for some reason forbidden for Firefox to remember it for me). In the course of doing the reset, the site not-so-conveniently trashed my comment. Gone. Zero. </p>
<p>I don&#8217;t care enough to write it twice if you can&#8217;t care enough to remember it once. If I write it again, it&#8217;ll go on my blog as part of my workflow series.</p>
