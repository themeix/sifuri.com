<p>Today&#8217;s NYT has a <a href="https://myaccount.nytimes.com/auth/login?URI=www-nc.nytimes.com/2009/04/10/us/politics/10morano.html&amp;REFUSE_COOKIE_ERROR=SHOW_ERROR">story</a> about a former Capitol Hill staffer who is a &#8220;dissenter&#8221; and &#8220;skeptic&#8221; on climate change issues. He might even be a &#8220;misinformer.&#8221; Congratulations to the NYT for breaking out the thesaurus and not calling the guy a &#8220;denier.&#8221; That term is loaded and hateful in its message.</p>
<p>I humbly submit Colosimo&#8217;s Corollary to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godwin%27s_law">Godwin&#8217;s Law</a>:</p>
<blockquote><p>Labeling someone a &#8220;denier&#8221; on any topic other than the Holocaust is a violation of Godwin&#8217;s Law.</p></blockquote>
<p>Do you have links to stories that share this view of the degradation in the climate change debate? It&#8217;s not about climate change, it&#8217;s about collegial debate on things that clearly have huge impacts on society but are almost certainly not inherently or deliberately evil (unlike say, Rwandan genocide or <a href="https://myaccount.nytimes.com/auth/login?URI=www-nc.nytimes.com/2009/02/06/nyregion/06kings.html&amp;REFUSE_COOKIE_ERROR=SHOW_ERROR">atrocious conditions in hospitals for the mentally ill</a>).</p>
