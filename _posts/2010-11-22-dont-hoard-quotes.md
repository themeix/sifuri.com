<p>I recently saw this <a href="http://lifehacker.com/5678432/quotables-collects-and-organizes-the-quotes-that-inspire-you">Lifehacker post</a> on <a href="http://quotabl.es/">Quotabl.es</a>, which is a standalone website that will collect and keep quotes that you like. It allows for tagging quotes and the [now-obligatory] ability to share on <a href="http://rickcolosimo.com/twitter/">Twitter</a> or <a href="http://rickcolosimo.com/facebook/">Facebook</a>.</p>
<p>But to/for me, this is the wrong approach! Why hoard quotes that are meaningful to you? Aren&#8217;t they important enough to share with the world? Just like no plan survives contact with the enemy, no idea spreads in a vacuum.</p>
<p>Tell people <a href="http://rickcolosimo.com/tag/quote/">who you are</a>, tell them what <a href="http://www.thoughtstorm.com/tag/quotes/">inspires</a> you, tell them what <a href="/tag/quotes/">moves</a> you. Don&#8217;t store &#8217;em in some antiseptic webapp: claim them, publish them (meaning someplace <em>public</em>, where people will find them), and share them.</p>
<p><em>(I think even Lifehacker <a href="http://lifehacker.com/t/quotables">understands</a> things my way.)</em></p>
