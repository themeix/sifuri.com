<p>This idea falls into my &#8220;Orphans&#8221; category: projects or ideas that I find interesting but that I know are really low on my priority list. I figure it&#8217;s more fun and helpful to write about them and release them into the wild. If nothing else, I can put a David-Allen approved &#8220;DONE&#8221; label on these in my mind.</p>
<p>This article about a <a href="https://uncrate.com/surfline-sessions/">personalized video of surfing lessons</a> reminded me of an idea I&#8217;d had a while ago.</p>
<p>When I&#8217;ve taken my kids to large amusement parks, a common feature these last 5&#8211;10 years has been the &#8220;photo pass.&#8221; The photo pass is an all-you-can-eat digital photo subscription. So, rather than pay $29 for a single photo of the three of us on a Harry Potter ride, we pay whatever &#8211; $99 or $129 &#8211; for several days&#8217; worth of all the photos that get taken, on rides or even the standup photographers near the entrance or other scenic spots (like in front of the Millenium Falcon!).</p>
<p>What I&#8217;d thought of some time ago was a similar, but better, value-added service that such a park could provide. With the number of both personal video sources (smartphones, tablets, and video cameras) and park-owned cameras of various kinds), the park could create a model where as part of the package, they provide me with a personal video camera, as well as a site to upload any other personal video (or pictures). Then, at the end of each day, I can turn in that camera to the park, and they get to work. They can take my video and maybe park-owned video that I appear in that fits a B-roll kind of definition &#8211; waiting in lines, passing security checkpoints in rides and attractions, eating lunch. There&#8217;s almost certainly lots of video available. And with Fastpass (at Disney) running on a GPS-enabled smartphone (and probably bluetooth tracking too), it should be easy to get a crowd shot with me in it.</p>
<p>The editing work could be done by a team of video editors in an off-cycle time zone 8 or 12 or 14 hours away, which might be convenient for many reasons. But here&#8217;s another idea: an AI tool that has part of the idea in place. Magisto (www.magisto.com) is supposed to be an AI-powered editing app. Their target is to help small businesses create marketing and product videos almost automagically. They take uploaded video and analyze and edit it to create a short piece.</p>
<p>Whether Disney takes all the footage and pushes it into an AI editor or has a team of folks across the world cleaning it up, it&#8217;s probably 2&#8211;3x better than any regular person&#8217;s editing and 1000x better than the editing that almost everyone WON&#8217;T DO FOR THEMSELF. That&#8217;s my point. Unless editing is your thing, a little bit of skill from someone else will lead to vastly better outcomes compared to what you probably won&#8217;t do.</p>
<p>And then I get to walk away with a good-looking video (on DVD too) and maybe a book of photos that won&#8217;t just live on my hard drive, never to be seen again. Grandparents (at least my parents) would be happier with a book they could look through than a link to an online album.</p>
