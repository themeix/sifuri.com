<p>I am currently attending a <a href="http://www.copaa.org/?">conference</a> related to special education law. This fact isn&#8217;t critically relevant to this post, but it explains why I was sitting at a table this morning watching the presenters try to get their laptops working and then wait for the inevitable &#8220;tech guy&#8221; from the hotel to come sort it out.</p>
<p>When you think for a few minutes about everything that we can do so easily &#8212; post phone videos directly to <a href="http://www.facebook.com/">Facebook</a>, use geosynchronous satellites to help us drive to the nearest <a href="http://www.starbucks.com/">Starbucks</a>, or publish our <a href="http://rickcolosimo.com/">thoughts</a> to the world using a sophisticated <a href="https://wordpress.org/">CMS</a> that non-techies can install on <a href="http://www.1and1.com/">shared servers</a> after 1/2 hour of reading &#8212; it&#8217;s amazing to me that, in conference rooms all over the world, it&#8217;s still hard to believe that companies haven&#8217;t settled on a straightforward plug &#8216;n&#8217; play solution. Why don&#8217;t all these systems just work? Plug in an external monitor cable, hit a key combo, and presto! I really can&#8217;t figure it out.</p>
